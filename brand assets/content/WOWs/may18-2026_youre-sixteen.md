## Content

Most artists of Ringo's stature in 1973 would have felt pressure to establish their originality with daring, boundary-pushing work. Ringo did the opposite. He covered a 1960 hit originally recorded by Johnny Burnette, delivered it with characteristic warmth and good humor, and watched it climb to the top of the American charts. "You're Sixteen" became his second U.S. number one because Ringo was not trying to be something he was not. He was simply being himself, completely and without apology.

Imitation in the hands of a genuine artist is not a lack of originality. It is a form of love, a tribute to material that moved you before you were famous enough for anyone to notice what moved you. Ringo grew up on this music. He loved it. His version of "You're Sixteen" sounds like someone playing their favorite song, and that feeling, that quality of genuine enthusiasm rather than performed professionalism, is precisely what made it connect.

Nature has a way of rewarding authenticity. We often believe that success requires a constant, exhausting effort to appear more impressive, more innovative, and more sophisticated than we actually are. Ringo spent his entire Beatles career navigating comparisons and underestimation, and his answer was always the same: just be yourself, and be that as fully as possible.

Enthusiasm, it turns out, is its own form of genius. When you bring genuine delight to whatever you are doing, whether it is an original composition or a beloved classic, that delight is contagious. Ringo's joy in this recording is palpable, and that joy is what made a thirteen-year-old song feel freshly alive. You do not always need a new idea. Sometimes you just need to bring your whole self to an existing one.

## Daily Challenge

**Today, I will bring genuine enthusiasm to something familiar rather than straining for novelty, trusting that my authentic engagement will make even ordinary moments extraordinary.**

## Reflection Questions

*Where in your life have you been undervaluing your natural enthusiasm in pursuit of something that seems more impressive? What would it mean to succeed simply by being fully, joyfully yourself?*
